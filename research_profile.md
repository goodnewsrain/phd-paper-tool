# 연구 프로필 (Research profile)

이 파일은 논문 요약 시 "내 연구와의 관련성"을 판단하는 **기준**으로 사용됩니다.
연구 방향이 바뀌면 이 파일만 수정하면 됩니다.

---

**Program / stance:** A practitioner-scholar in a **Leadership, Equity, and Inquiry** doctoral program, studying the very institution they work inside (mid-level executive in a regional governing body of a large mainline denomination).

**Core research question:** How do the **governance structures of a large national faith-based nonprofit** (e.g., the Presbyterian Church (USA)) **reproduce racial and cultural inequality across its regional governing bodies / affiliates** — through governance thresholds, resource-allocation models, and leadership pipelines — and what does **multi-site comparative analysis** of those regional bodies reveal about policy/program interventions that can produce genuine **equity and belonging** for minority-led (immigrant, BIPOC) communities at the **national scale**?

**Key themes & concepts:**
- **Racialized organizations theory** — how ostensibly neutral policies distribute power and resources unequally; how inequality is reproduced across institutional levels without explicit intent.
- Critical organizational theory; institutional / structural inequality.
- **Diversity (celebrated) vs. belonging (structurally shared)**; symbolic vs. structural DEI; DEI as an *institutional capacity*, not a personal virtue.
- **Procedural neutrality preserving unequal status** in predominantly white institutions; "equal rules" vs. equitable outcomes.
- Governance thresholds, resource allocation, informal leadership networks/pipelines as mechanisms of inequality.
- Intercultural / immigrant & minority leadership; marginality as an epistemic vantage (Jung Young Lee).
- Nonprofit / denominational / faith-based governance; equity leadership; organizational change.

**Methods of interest:** multi-site comparative **case studies** of regional governing bodies; qualitative inquiry in applied settings; **participatory / community-involved research** (affected communities shape the questions, not just answer them); building an evidence base rigorous enough for national-level policy claims.

**When judging relevance, consider** how the paper informs: institutional/organizational inequality, race & power in organizations, governance and equity, nonprofit/faith-based/denominational governance, DEI implementation and its limits, leadership pipelines, or the research methods above. Papers far from these (unrelated technical/scientific fields) are Low relevance even if excellent.
